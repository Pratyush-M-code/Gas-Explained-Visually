Volume

Introduction to Volume

Volume is the amount of space the particles can take up! Here, we've represented particles by small balls so that it's not only easier to understand but also intuitive!
For solids, their volume is simply the space taken up by their particles and is fixed.
Try to click and drag the solid simulation to see how the particles behave! As you may have noticed, though the shape of the object may change, the overall volume remains the same.
P.S. This is a soft solid, in reality a hard solid would resist far more.

Volume of liquid

In liquids, their many particles are constantly sloshing around and moving, and they have no fixed shape. This is due to a weaker force of attraction between the molecules of the liquid! Hence, a liquid's volume is the capacity of a container required to hold the liquid.
Check out the simulation; it depicts a low-viscosity (willing to flow) liquid. See how it flows and is never truly stationary.
Use the slider to change the width of the container. Also, observe that the liquid changes its shape to fit the container it is stored in.

Gaseous Volumes

In gases, particles move freely and rapidly in all directions, with very weak forces of attraction between them. This allows gases to have neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.
In this simulation, the slider changes the overall size of the container. Observe how all particles move and spread randomly until they have filled the available space.
Unlike solids and liquids, gases are highly compressible due to their empty spaces. So, we are able to change their volume quite easily as compared to solids and liquids.
This means that the volume of a gas depends on the size of its container!
