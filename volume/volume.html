Volume

Introduction to Volume

Volume is the amount of space the particles can take up! Here, we've represented particles by small balls so that it's not only easier to understand but also intuitive!
For solids, their volume is simply the space taken up by their particles and is fixed.
Try to click and drag the solid simulation to see how the particles behave! As you may have noticed, though the shape of the object may change, the overall volume remains the same.

Volume of liquid

In liquids, their many particles are constantly sloshing around and moving, and they have no fixed shape. This is due to a weaker force of attraction between the molecules of the liquid! Hence, a liquid's volume is the capacity of a container required to hold the liquid.
Check out the simulation; it depicts a low-viscosity (willing to flow) liquid. See how it flows and is never truly stationary. Also, observe that its shape fits the container it is stored in.

Gasous Volumes

Since gases do not have a volume of their own, they occupy the entire volume of the container in which they are kept; therefore, the volume of the container is taken as the volume of the gas
