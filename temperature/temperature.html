Temperature

What is Temperature?

To put it simply, temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of a substance.
However, we can also look at the effect temperature has at the microscopic scale; this is known as thermal energy. Heating a substance introduces more thermal energy into the substance, while cooling reduces thermal energy. The most observed event that is a result of thermal energy is the phase change of substances, i.e. changing of a substance from liquid to gas or from solid to liquid and vice versa.
Phase change occurs when the substance's molecules gain enough energy to break free from the attractive energy that keeps them bound. In solids, the energy to cause this change is called enthalpy of fusion and in liquids it is called enthalpy of vaporization.

Temperature as a Measure of Internal Energy

So, what does Temperature actually measure?
In general, Temperature is a measure of the Avg Kinetic Energy per particles. This means when we heat or cool a substance, we are actually increasing the kinetic energy of the molecules.
This is different from internal energy, which is the total kinetic energy of every particle combined. Temperature isn't affected by the amount of particles, only by how fast each one is moving on average. Internal energy accounts for both.
Hence given that the amount of particles are fixed for these simulations, temperature can be used as a measure of internal energy!
